# Pack setup: what to extract

Read this after the sources are known and before reading deep. All of it stays inside the read caps in the skill.

## From the CRM

Pull the open deals (owner, last activity, days in stage, close date, amount, stage), along with deals won or lost in the window, stage names for each sales pipeline, the titles of contacts and types of companies, products and amounts, and notes up to the note budget. From these, build:

1. The actual sales process: where leads originate, how the first call goes, when price comes up, who joins and when, and what prompts a close.
2. Conversion and days per stage, plus what actually happened before a deal moved (from notes, calls, emails). Those observed events become the entry proof in deal-lifecycle. If stage entry dates are missing, use time from creation to close and say so.
3. The qualification method in use (MEDDPICC, SPICED, BANT, or the team's own), which fields fill in reliably, and which are often empty.
4. What lost deals are missing that won deals have (a business case ahead of pricing, a recap following the demo, a champion, multi-threading), and the point at which lost deals die.
5. Typical deal size, cycle length, and win rate.

## From calls

Start from recent summaries and the 3 full transcripts. Look for how the person opens, the discovery questions they ask, how they explain the product, how they handle price and objections, which competitors come up and what is said about them, how rapport and tension are handled, and how the call is closed and the next step set. Keep real quotes.

## From email (voice)

Use the 25 external buyer threads and the replies. Exclude internal mail, newsletters, sequence or template mail, and anything a tool appended. Record whether the email tool adds a signature so drafts do not repeat it.

Capture:
- Mechanics: the precise greeting and closing, subject lines, how long sentences and paragraphs run, formality, humor, emoji, exclamation marks, and whether bullets or prose are used.
- Structure: whether the ask comes first or builds up, how it closes, how many asks per email.
- By situation (first follow-up, recap after a demo, nudge on a quiet deal, pricing and proposal, scheduling, short reply): how the tone shifts, with one or two real examples each.
- Five to eight of the person's own phrases, word for word.
- What separates replied-to emails from ignored ones (length, subject, closing question). Say this only when each group holds at least 8 emails, and give both totals. Never write "twice as likely" or similar.
- Always and never rules.

## Best-deal reading

Read stage changes, every email in both directions, every call, who joined and when, and time per stage. Pick 3 to 5 behaviors that differ from an ordinary deal: speed of follow-up, what was sent after the demo, how and when threading happened, how price was handled, the champion, the close.

## Working-style evidence

Build every section from evidence and never ask open questions to fill it.
- Moves from my best deals: from the best-deal analysis, the won deals, and what the best calls share.
- Time sinks: from timestamps (CRM updated days after a call, late or missing recaps, long pre-meeting research, repeated nudges on one deal).
- Recurring slips: moves present in the best deals that most other deals lack.
- Standing rules: the always and never patterns in email and calls.
- Calendar shows meeting types and volume; the company site shows positioning and case-study pages.

## Role signals

Check in order of strength: CRM ownership (per the shared rules); the CRM's team, role and manager fields; job title (signature, CRM user record, quick web lookup); the calendar (recurring one-on-ones, standups for the team, reviews of pipeline or forecast); and finally email and chat (deal reviews, coaching, a rep forwarding a thread). Compare the user's email with the CRM owners: no match and zero owned deals means leader, and the summary says so. When signals conflict or are absent, do not stop. Turn it into one of the two summary questions.

For a leader or both, go through the team's deals by owner, noting each rep's conversion and days per stage, SDR to AE and AE to CS handoffs, differences in pipeline and activity from rep to rep, the standout habit with counts, and the points where the team's process fails.
